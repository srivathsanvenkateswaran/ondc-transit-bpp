## 1. The Karnataka RTC split (read this before the class list — it governs everything else)

Karnataka has **four** state-owned road transport corporations, not one **[V - Wikipedia/kkrtc.karnataka.gov.in]**:

| Corporation | HQ | Territory | Formed |
|---|---|---|---|
| **BMTC** (Bangalore Metropolitan Transport Corporation) | Bengaluru | Bengaluru city only | 15 Aug 1997, carved out of KSRTC |
| **KSRTC** (Karnataka State Road Transport Corporation) | Bengaluru | Southern Karnataka excluding Bengaluru city; the "parent" corporation | Original body; predates the splits |
| **NWKRTC** (North Western Karnataka RTC) | Hubballi | North-western Karnataka | 1 Nov 1997, carved out of KSRTC |
| **KKRTC** (Kalyana Karnataka RTC) | Kalaburagi | Kalaburagi, Bidar, Yadgir, Raichur, Koppal, Ballari, Vijayanagara districts | Est. 15 Aug 2000 as **NEKRTC** (North Eastern Karnataka RTC); renamed **KKRTC** by gubernatorial order on 6 July 2021, following the 2019 renaming of the Hyderabad-Karnataka region to Kalyana Karnataka |

**Bengaluru → Hampi/Hosapete is Kalyana Karnataka territory.** Hosapete (Hospet) sits in Ballari district; Hampi itself is in Vijayanagara district (carved from Ballari in 2021) — both are KKRTC jurisdiction, not KSRTC's **[V]**. In practice this matters less than it sounds: KSRTC, NWKRTC and KKRTC share a **single reservation brand and portal** (AWATAR, at ksrtc.in), and premium classes (Airavat, Ambaari, Rajahamsa, Pallakki) are run jointly by KSRTC + NWKRTC + KKRTC under one naming scheme, each corporation contributing its own buses to shared route brands **[V - Wikipedia infoboxes, kkrtc.karnataka.gov.in]**. So a Bengaluru–Hampi overnight bus booked as "KSRTC" on Redbus/AbhiBus may actually be a KKRTC-owned bus running on a route that originates in KSRTC territory — aggregators and even KSRTC's own booking site do not surface which corporation physically owns the specific bus **[I]**. For Tatak's purposes: the operating brand a rider sees ("KSRTC") is not a reliable indicator of which of the four corporations actually dispatched the vehicle.

Only BMTC is out of scope for "intercity KSRTC" — its Vajra/Vayu Vajra fleet is city-limited. Nandi Hills sits in Chikkaballapur district, also KSRTC (not BMTC) territory — see §2.

## 3. The three named corridors

### Mysuru → Madikeri (Kodagu/Coorg)
- **Distance/time**: ~118–120 km, roughly 3–4 hours by road **[S]**.
- **Frequency**: Described as a bus roughly every 30–45 minutes from Madikeri bus stand across KSRTC + private operators combined — I could not isolate a KSRTC-only frequency figure **[S]**.
- **Classes**: This corridor runs on ordinary/express seating, not premium sleeper classes — it's a short daytime hill route, not an overnight run. One search snippet found "0 buses currently operated by KSRTC" on an aggregator for this exact city pair on the day queried, which may reflect real-time inventory rather than route non-existence (see §6 — availability ≠ existence) **[S, caveat noted]**. KSRTC also runs a "Fly Bus" premium service connecting Bengaluru airport to Mysuru/Madikeri/Coimbatore/Kundapura **[S]**.
- **Fare**: One aggregator listed ~₹495 for a KSRTC fare on this pair; broader market range across all operators ₹166–₹2,120 depending on class **[S]**. Sitting-only is consistent with the short journey length; I found no sleeper service advertised on this specific city pair.

### Bengaluru → Chennai
- **Distance/time**: 347 km, ~8 hours **[S — AbhiBus summary]**.
- **Classes running**: Airavat, Airavat Club Class (Volvo AC multi-axle semi-sleeper, 2+2, 53 seats), Ambaari Utsav (Volvo 9600S AC sleeper, 2+1), Rajahamsa Executive (non-AC ultra-deluxe seater, 2+2), and Non-AC Sleeper **[S]**.
- **Reported schedule**: three KSRTC departures cited at 10:00 am, 9:55 pm, 10:15 pm — this is from a single aggregator summary and is very likely a subset, not the full timetable **[S, low confidence on completeness]**.
- **Inter-state counterpart**: **SETC** (State Express Transport Corporation), the long-distance arm of **TNSTC** (Tamil Nadu State Transport Corporation), runs the Tamil Nadu side of the same corridor with AC seater-sleeper and AC sleeper buses; SETC's charter is specifically routes over 300 km connecting Tamil Nadu district HQs to neighbouring states (Karnataka, AP, Kerala, Puducherry) **[V - Wikipedia/State Express Transport Corporation]**. Bengaluru–Chennai is a genuinely bilateral corridor: both KSRTC (Karnataka side) and SETC/TNSTC (Tamil Nadu side) sell tickets on it as separate operators, each visible independently on redBus/AbhiBus.
- **Boarding points in Chennai** (from the Karnataka side's listed pickup points) include Adambakkam, Adyar, Alandur, Ambattur, Anna Nagar, Anna University, Ashok Nagar, and others spread across the city — i.e., boarding/dropping is genuinely multi-point, not a single terminus **[S]**. I did not get an equally granular Bengaluru-side boarding-point list beyond "Majestic," though other sourcing (redBus route data generally) plus the prompt's own example (Majestic → Madiwala → Electronic City) is consistent with standard Karnataka-RTC practice of multiple city pickup points before the highway.
- **Fare band**: no clean multi-class fare table found; anecdotal/aggregator figures put non-AC sleeper around ₹475–₹2,000+ across all operators (not KSRTC-specific) **[S, weak]**.

### Bengaluru → Hampi / Hosapete
- **Operating corporation**: Booked and branded as "KSRTC" on ksrtc.in and every aggregator, but the destination end (Hosapete/Ballari, and Hampi/Vijayanagara) is KKRTC territory (§1). The Pallakki-class product itself is explicitly a joint KSRTC+NWKRTC+KKRTC brand **[V/S — see class table]**. I could not confirm from a primary source whether the specific nightly Majestic-origin bus is a KSRTC-owned vehicle or a KKRTC vehicle sold under the shared brand; treat "which corporation" as administratively ambiguous rather than cleanly "KSRTC."
- **Class mix**: Non-AC Sleeper "Pallakki" (2+1, 30 berths) is the flagship overnight product; AC Sleeper (2+1, Ashok Leyland/Volvo) options also list on aggregators for this route **[S]**.
- **Overnight sleeper reality — confirmed with a specific timetable**: Route 2259BNGHMP departs Majestic Bus Station at 10:59 pm daily, reaches Hosapete by ~6:00 am, and reaches Hampi by ~6:30 am — a genuine overnight sleeper run, ~7.5 hours, 9 stops including Kudlagi **[S — ksrtcbus.in route page, via search summary; primary fetch of ksrtcbus.in failed with DNS error so this is not independently re-verified]**. Return service 2001HMPBNG departs Hampi 8:00 pm, transfers at Hospet (halts, departs 8:30 pm), reaches Majestic ~4:30 am **[S, same caveat]**.
- **Drop point**: The service explicitly distinguishes Hosapete (a mid-route stop, ~6:00 am) from Hampi village itself (final stop, ~6:30 am) — i.e., it does drop in Hampi village, not just Hosapete, contrary to an assumption that KSRTC buses stop short at Hosapete and require a local transfer **[S]**.
- **Fare band**: aggregator "cheapest fare" figures of ₹450–₹606 for Bengaluru–Hampi and ₹570+ for Bengaluru–Hosapete were reported, but these read like non-AC or promotional floor prices rather than a full fare table across classes **[S, weak]**.

### Bengaluru → Nandi Hills
This is a genuine gap worth flagging plainly: **there is a KSRTC service, but it is thin, not a proper "route" in the intercity sense.**
- One search result states KSRTC runs a **daily bus from Majestic depot to Nandi Hills**, departing around 8:30 am, fare ₹50–₹200, described explicitly as "the only direct bus of the day" **[S — low-confidence tourism-blog sourcing, not a KSRTC timetable page]**.
- The commonly cited alternative is indirect: KSRTC/BMTC to Chikkaballapur (~₹42), then a separate local bus up the hill (~₹30) **[S]**.
- I found no BMTC service to Nandi Hills in this research (BMTC's own premium routes are airport/city-focused); this appears to be a KSRTC ordinary-bus problem, not a BMTC one, but the "own vehicle is the realistic way most people do this" framing that's common in travel forums is consistent with what I found — a single daily direct departure is not a reliable transit option to model as "the way to get to Nandi Hills." **This should be treated as a genuine data/service thinness, not a Tatak coverage failure**: even if KSRTC's schedule were perfectly ingested, "one bus a day, unclear reliability, ambiguous whether it still runs" is the ground truth, not an artifact of missing data.

## 4. Data availability — the critical question

**Bottom line: no open, machine-readable schedule dataset exists for KSRTC (or NWKRTC/KKRTC/NEKRTC) intercity services, as of this research.** Specifically:

- **GTFS**: KSRTC's own FAQ page (mitra.ksrtc.in) states that KSRTC data is made available "to application developers who agree to provisions of an access agreement," in GTFS format, covering AGENCY/ROUTE/STOPTIMES — but this describes a *gated, agreement-based* program, and my direct fetch of that FAQ page failed (DNS/connection error against `mitra.ksrtc.in`), so I could not confirm the program is still live or how to actually apply **[S, unverified — could not independently reach the primary page]**.
- **World Bank assessment**: A 2020 World Bank document, "Open Transport Data Assessment in Mysore," recommended that KSRTC "prepare a section on its website where GTFS feeds can be distributed" — phrased as a recommendation, implying it did not exist as of that report; I could not extract readable text from the PDF to confirm current status **[S — WebFetch could not parse the PDF; treat as background, not current-state proof]**. The clear signal from the broader search: **"Currently, there is no open data platform in the state of Karnataka"** for transit **[S]**.
- **data.gov.in**: There is a listed department page for KSRTC on data.gov.in, but I found no evidence — in the page listing or in search results — of an actual schedule/GTFS/timetable dataset published there. My WebFetch of the page itself returned HTTP 403, so I could not enumerate its datasets directly **[unverified — fetch blocked]**.
- **Community scrape equivalent to Vonter/bmtc-gtfs**: **None found.** `Vonter/bmtc-gtfs` (github.com/Vonter/bmtc-gtfs) is a real, active unofficial GTFS dataset — but it is BMTC-only (city bus), built by reverse-engineering the Namma BMTC app's live-tracking API, and explicitly excludes routes without live GPS tracking **[V — GitHub repo confirmed]**. I found no equivalent project for KSRTC/NWKRTC/KKRTC intercity schedules — no GitHub repo, no published API reverse-engineering writeup, nothing. This is a real gap, not a search-failure artifact: KSRTC intercity buses do not appear to have the kind of public live-tracking API that made the BMTC scrape possible in the first place.
- **KSRTC's own reservation API (Awatar)**: exists and is what ksrtc.in, the KSRTC AWATAR app, and (per redBus's own claim of being an "official partner of all government bus services") redBus/AbhiBus/Yatra/Goibibo/MakeMyTrip all sit on top of. But this is a **booking-transaction API reachable only through commercial partnership agreements**, not a public read-only schedule feed. No published, self-serve API documentation for it turned up in this research.
- **ONDC / Beckn mobility network — the most concrete finding in this section**: ONDC's public transport ticketing is real and growing, but it is **metro and intra-city bus only**, not intercity. As of the most recent figures found (~June 2026): metro ticketing live in Bengaluru, Chennai, Delhi, Kochi, Pune, Nagpur, and all three Mumbai Metro lines; intra-city bus ticketing live for DTC (Delhi), CRUT (Odisha), BEST (Mumbai), and Katch Mobility — over 300,000–370,000 trips/day across ~30+ buyer apps (Chalo, Tummoc, One Delhi, Katchapp, Chartr, redBus, Uber, etc.) **[V — Business Standard, startuptalky.com]**. Crucially, the Beckn/ONDC domain-code registry lists **TRV11 = "Unreserved Ticket Booking (Metro/Intracity Bus)"**, already released (v2.0.0), and **TRV12 = "Intercity Bus Ticket Booking"** — but still in **draft status** ("draft-TRV12-intercity"), i.e., not yet a released, live domain on the network **[S — search-summarized from schema.beckn.io indexing; I could not directly load schema.beckn.io/schema.nfh.global to re-verify the raw registry entry, so this should be treated as reported-but-not-independently-refetched, not confirmed against the primary schema page]**. No KSRTC, NWKRTC, KKRTC, or any other state RTC intercity operator appears in any live ONDC participant list I found.

**Net assessment for Tatak's honesty contract**: there is no dataset, official or scraped, that would let Tatak show a real KSRTC intercity departure time without either (a) a commercial data-partnership with KSRTC/redBus-class aggregators, or (b) building and maintaining its own scraper against a booking site's undocumented API (which is what Vonter did for BMTC, and no equivalent exists yet for KSRTC). Any KSRTC intercity feature that promises real departure times today would be promising something the domain currently cannot back up from any public source.

## 5. Seat inventory and reservation mechanics

- **Advance booking window**: 30 days ahead, excluding the day of departure; reservations close 30–45 minutes before departure ordinarily, longer for some late-night/major-city routes **[V — ksrtc.in/reservation_terms]**.
- **Cancellation/refund slabs** (KSRTC official) **[V]**:
  - >72 hours before departure: 10% of base fare deducted
  - 72–24 hours before: 25% deducted
  - 24–2 hours before: 50% deducted
  - <2 hours before or after departure: no refund
  - Reservation fee is always non-refundable; toll/bridge fees are refunded in full regardless of slab.
- **Concessions** **[V]**: Senior citizens (60+, Karnataka residents only) get 25% off basic fare on Rajahamsa and lower classes, ID required. Children ≤6 travel free; 6–12 get 50–75% off depending on service class; >12 pay full adult fare. Separately, the **Shakti Scheme** (launched 11 June 2023) gives Karnataka-resident women (and gender minorities) free travel on ordinary/express services only — explicitly excluding every premium class: Rajahamsa, Non-AC/AC Streepar, Airavat, Airavat Club Class, Ambaari/Ambaari Dream/Ambaari Utsav, Fly Bus, EV Power Plus **[V — bengaluruurban.nic.in]**. This is a first-class fact for Tatak: "free for women" is a real, common, and *class-conditional* rule, not a flat policy.
- **Gender-based seat rules**: The AWATAR booking app has a "Single Lady" checkbox; when selected it restricts the adjacent seat to female booking only. Applies across most service classes but **excludes Karnataka Sarige and BMTC Volvo services** per the T&C summary found **[S]**.
- **Ticket format**: Counter bookings get KSRTC pre-printed stationery; online/app/franchisee bookings get an A4 printout, SMS m-ticket, or a phone-displayed QR/image **[V]**. Carrying a physical photo ID matching the e-ticket/m-ticket is mandatory — driving licence, voter ID, passport, Aadhaar, or other government photo ID **[V]**.
- **Luggage**: 30 kg free personal luggage (15 kg for a chargeable child ticket); KSRTC disclaims liability for valuables (electronics, jewellery, cash) **[V]**.
- **Boarding point changes**: allowed for counter/franchisee bookings up until advance-booking closes; **not permitted for online bookings** **[V]**. This is directly relevant to any "change your boarding point" UX Tatak might consider — it's not universally possible.
- **Seat hold/lock and race conditions**: not KSRTC-specific data, but general Indian bus-aggregator practice, sourced from redBus: seats are locked for roughly 10–15 minutes during checkout, and failed-payment seats become available again within that window (varies by payment method, up to 30 minutes for pay-at-store) **[S — Quora/security-research summary, not an official redBus statement]**. A LinkedIn technical post describes redBus using "pessimistic locking" to prevent double-booking, i.e., a seat is exclusively locked to one checkout session for its hold window rather than allowing optimistic/last-write-wins concurrency **[S, single secondary source]**. I found no KSRTC-specific documentation of its own AWATAR seat-lock duration; assume something similar exists but is undocumented publicly **[I]**.
- **GST**: AC bus tickets attract 5% GST (SAC code 996411) without input tax credit, or operators can opt for 12% GST with full ITC eligibility. Non-AC bus tickets are GST-exempt **[S — multiple tax-advisory sites, consistent with each other, not a primary CBIC citation]**. This matters for any fare breakdown Tatak might show: AC-class KSRTC tickets (Airavat, Ambaari, etc.) carry a tax component that ordinary/non-AC Karnataka Sarige and Pallakki tickets do not.
- **Fare rules — distance slab vs point-to-point table**: I found no direct evidence resolving this either way for KSRTC specifically. The existence of differentiated multi-point boarding fares on the Bengaluru–Chennai corridor (different Chennai-side pickup points listed separately) strongly implies KSRTC/aggregators price by point-to-point pairs rather than a pure distance formula, since a distance-only model would need real per-point distances anyway — but I could not find an official published fare table (distance slab or otherwise) confirming the calculation method **[I]**.

## 6. How intercity differs from intra-city for a journey planner

These are structural differences a Tatak intercity feature must model, not just fare/time changes:

- **Seat availability is a first-class travel constraint.** A BMTC bus or metro train "exists" on a route regardless of load — you queue and board. A KSRTC intercity service, especially a once-daily overnight sleeper (Pallakki class Bengaluru→Hampi, or the single daily Nandi Hills bus), can be **fully booked days out**. "Route exists" and "you can travel today" are different facts, and only the second one is what a traveller actually needs answered. This is the single biggest conceptual departure from BMTC/Metro planning, where Tatak's honesty contract is about *times*; for KSRTC intercity it must also be about *availability*.
- **Once-a-day (or few-times-a-day) services are common**, not the exception — confirmed concretely by the Bengaluru–Hampi Pallakki route (one nightly departure each direction) and the Nandi Hills route (one direct bus a day). Missing that one departure is a full-day (or overnight) delay, unlike intra-city routes with headway-based service.
- **Multi-day journeys and overnight travel** are real trip shapes intercity introduces: the traveller may sleep on the bus (hence sleeper classes existing at all), and "journey time" spans a date boundary — a planner showing only same-day results would misrepresent an overnight sleeper trip.
- **Boarding point ≠ origin, and it changes both fare and departure time.** Confirmed for Bengaluru–Chennai (many distinct city-side pickup points feeding into one physical bus) and implied by the general online-booking rule that boarding point is fixed at booking time and can't be changed for online tickets. A traveller boarding at, say, Electronic City on a Bengaluru→Chennai service departs later and possibly pays a different fare than one boarding at Majestic — Tatak cannot treat "the bus's departure time" as a single value independent of where the rider boards.
- **Advance booking vs walk-up is a real behavioural split** the planner should account for: KSRTC allows booking up to 30 days ahead and closes 30–45 minutes before departure, but walk-up/counter booking coexists right up to near-departure. A journey planner recommending "board this KSRTC bus" without surfacing whether inventory is actually open (or whether the trip requires pre-booking days in advance for a popular overnight route) risks the same honesty-contract violation Tatak already avoids for BMTC/Metro — telling someone a time exists when a seat does not.
- **A composite trip decomposes across operators and modes with a hard interchange point.** The prompt's own example — Kundalahalli Gate → Hampi — genuinely decomposes as: BMTC feeder route (e.g. 500F) → Namma Metro → Majestic (Kempegowda Bus Station, adjacent to/near the metro interchange) → KSRTC overnight intercity leg to Hosapete/Hampi. Each leg is a different operator, different booking system (BMTC/Metro pay-as-you-go vs KSRTC advance-reserved seat), and the last leg is the one where seat availability, not just schedule, gates whether the trip is even possible on a given day. A planner that stitches legs together on schedule alone, without flagging that the KSRTC leg needs its own reservation and may not have a seat, would be technically accurate about times and still misleading about feasibility.
