## 1. The five operators, sized (detail)

### BMTC — Bangalore Metropolitan Transport Corporation
- Fleet ~7,067 buses, >1,700 electric (July 2026 figure) [S] (bengalurumetro.in / sustainable-bus.com aggregation)
- 5,729 distinct routes, 50 depots [S]
- Daily ridership ~48 lakh (Oct 2025) [S]; ~61,000 trips/day, ~1.19M km/day [S]
- Operating area: Bengaluru city core plus ~25 km outward, touching Ramanagara and Chikkaballapura [S]
- Pure city operator — no mofussil, no interstate.
- Split from KSRTC in August 1997 [S].

### KSRTC — Karnataka State Road Transport Corporation
- Fleet: sources disagree — "more than 25,000 vehicles" (Wikipedia infobox, likely stale/aggregated or wrong), vs. 8,663–8,893 buses in other citations [S]. The 8,600–8,900 range is corroborated by two independent pulls and is the one to trust; 25,000 looks like an infobox error (possibly conflating cumulative fleet ever operated, or a transcription of the pre-1997 undivided corporation's fleet).
- 82–83 depots, 170 bus stations, 17 operating divisions + 1 bus-station division (Kempegowda) [S]
- Workforce ~34,000–38,000 [S]
- Daily ridership ~34.7 lakh, ~17.2–29 lakh km/day (sources disagree on km/day, not ridership) [S]
- Districts: the residual territory after BMTC (1997), NWKRTC (1997) and NEKRTC/KKRTC (2000) were carved out — the south and center: Bengaluru Urban/Rural, Ramanagara, Kolar, Chikkaballapura, Tumakuru, Chitradurga, Davanagere, Shivamogga, Dakshina Kannada (Mangaluru), Udupi, Chikmagalur, Hassan, Mysuru, Mandya, Chamarajanagar, Kodagu [S]
- Also runs Mysuru city bus service as a KSRTC "Urban Division" out of Mysuru CBS — not a separate corporation [S][I]
- Runs the AWATAR reservation system, which is the shared advance-booking backbone for KSRTC + NWKRTC + KKRTC (and some BMTC services) [S]
- Service classes span everything from basic non-AC to Airavat Club/Gold, Rajahamsa, Ambaari, Pallakki sleeper, EV-Power Plus — city, mofussil, intercity, interstate and premium all under one corporation.

### NWKRTC — North Western Karnataka Road Transport Corporation
- Fleet ~5,000–5,398 buses (sources vary) [S]
- ~4,190–4,440 routes/schedules, reaching 4,428+ villages across 44 talukas [S]
- 55–56 depots, 176 bus stations, 9 divisions (Belagavi, Hubballi, Dharwad, Sirsi, Bagalkot, Gadag, Chikkodi, Haveri, Hubballi-Dharwad City) [S]
- Workforce ~22,000 [S]
- Daily ridership ~25.9 lakh, ~1.55M km/day [S]
- Districts: Belagavi, Dharwad, Uttara Kannada, Bagalkot, Gadag, Haveri [S] — routes extend into ~24 more intrastate districts plus 5 neighboring states (Goa, Maharashtra) for interstate service [S]
- Operates Hubballi-Dharwad city services and jointly runs the Chigari BRTS (see §3).
- HQ Hubballi. Formed Nov 1997.

### KKRTC — Kalyana Karnataka Road Transport Corporation (formerly NEKRTC)
- Renamed from North Eastern Karnataka Road Transport Corporation on **6 July 2021** [V] (matches the region's own 2019 rename from "Hyderabad-Karnataka" to "Kalyana Karnataka" under the 371(J) special-status framework) — confirmed current legal/operating name is **Kalyana Karnataka Road Transport Corporation**.
- Founded 15 Aug 2000, bifurcated out of KSRTC [S]
- Fleet ~4,531–4,700 buses (sources vary) [S]
- 52–53 depots, 152–154 bus stands, 9 divisions (Ballari, Bidar, Bijapur, Hospet, Kalaburagi-01, Kalaburagi-02, Koppal, Raichur, Yadgir) [S]
- Workforce ~19,430 [S]
- Daily ridership ~12.3 lakh [S]
- **Districts confirmed**: Kalaburagi (HQ), Bijapur (Vijayapura), Bidar, Yadgir, Raichur, Koppal, **Ballari**, **Vijayanagara** — 8 districts [V, cross-checked across two independent fetches]. Note: one search result also listed KKRTC as covering a much larger list including Bengaluru, Mysuru, Dharwad, Belagavi etc. — that appears to be conflating "districts served by *some* KKRTC interstate/inter-division route" with "districts in KKRTC's operating jurisdiction." Treat the 8-district list as the actual base territory [I]; the wider list reflects long-haul routes only, the same way KSRTC buses reach Goa without Goa being KSRTC territory.
- Interstate reach: Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Maharashtra, Goa, Tamil Nadu [S]
- Bus classes shared with KSRTC/NWKRTC naming (Rajahamsa, Ambaari, Pallakki), plus KKRTC-specific "Kalyana Ratha" sleeper.

### BMRCL — Bangalore Metro Rail Corporation Limited (Namma Metro)
- JV of Government of India and Government of Karnataka [V]
- 3 operational lines, 83 stations total (74 elevated, 8 underground, 1 at-grade), ~96 km [S]
  - Purple: 43.49 km, 37 stations, Challaghatta–Whitefield
  - Green: 33.46 km, 32 stations, Silk Institute–Madavara
  - Yellow: 19.15 km, 16 stations, RV Road–Bommasandra, opened 10 Aug 2025
- Daily ridership ~9.16 lakh (Apr 2026), record 10.48 lakh (Aug 2025) [S]; annual ridership 232.8M in 2023 [S]
- Pink Line (21.25 km, 18 stations: 6 elevated, 12 underground): CMRS inspection concluded 14 Aug 2026, signalling ISA certificate pending, opening expected late Aug/early Sep 2026 for the elevated section, underground section targeted Dec 2026 [S] — **not yet open as of report date (5 Sep 2026)**, so treat as imminent but unconfirmed.
- Blue Line (58.19 km, 32 stations, airport connector): under construction, Phase 2A (Silk Board–KR Puram) and 2B (KR Puram–airport), targeted completion March 2028 [S]
- Ticketing: Contactless Smart Tokens (single journey), Contactless Smart Cards, QR-code mobile ticketing, NCMC support since 2023 [S]

## 2. The territorial split, precisely

Karnataka's road transport was undivided KSRTC until 1997, then split three times:
1. **1997**: BMTC carved out for Bengaluru city.
2. **1997**: NWKRTC carved out for the northwest.
3. **2000**: NEKRTC carved out for the northeast, renamed **KKRTC** in **2021**.

Resulting territory, district by district:

- **BMTC**: Bengaluru Urban core + fringe reach into Bengaluru Rural, Ramanagara, Chikkaballapura, Kolar.
- **NWKRTC**: Belagavi, Dharwad, Uttara Kannada, Bagalkot, Gadag, Haveri.
- **KKRTC**: Kalaburagi, Vijayapura (Bijapur), Bidar, Yadgir, Raichur, Koppal, Ballari, **Vijayanagara** (which contains Hampi/Hosapete — created in 2020–21 by splitting Ballari district).
- **KSRTC**: everything left over — Bengaluru Urban/Rural (secondary, alongside BMTC), Ramanagara, Kolar, Chikkaballapura, Tumakuru, Chitradurga, Davanagere, Shivamogga, Dakshina Kannada, Udupi, Chikmagalur, Hassan, Mysuru, Mandya, Chamarajanagar, Kodagu.

That's 30 districts (Karnataka's 2023 count after Vijayanagara's creation) covered, with overlap concentrated around Bengaluru (BMTC + KSRTC both operate there — KSRTC runs the long-distance and interstate services out of Bengaluru's bus stations while BMTC runs city routes) [I, standard practice, not separately verified] and around district boundaries where a corporation's home-division buses cross into a neighbor's territory on their way through (e.g., NWKRTC buses transiting Davanagere/Chitradurga en route from Hubballi to Bengaluru).

**Interstate allocation**: each corporation runs its own interstate services from its own territory outward — KSRTC to Tamil Nadu/Kerala/Andhra/Telangana/Maharashtra/Goa/Puducherry from the south; NWKRTC to Goa/Maharashtra from the northwest; KKRTC to Andhra/Telangana/Maharashtra/Goa/Tamil Nadu from the northeast. There's no single allocation rule beyond "the corporation whose territory borders that state runs the crossing" — this is [I], not documented as a formal policy anywhere found.

### Bengaluru → Hampi: which corporation, and does anyone tell you?

Hampi sits in **Vijayanagara district**, which is **KKRTC territory** by the confirmed 8-district list above. The nearest large town, Hosapete (Hospet), is also Vijayanagara district / KKRTC territory.

But every consumer-facing source found (KSRTC's own retail booking, AbhiBus, Asianet News coverage of the "Pallakki" sleeper service) reports the Bengaluru–Hampi/Hospet overnight bus as a **KSRTC** service, run from Bengaluru's Majestic bus station. [S]

The likely explanation, not independently confirmed: KSRTC (the state's largest and Bengaluru-based corporation) runs this route as one of its long-haul services originating from its own territory and terminating in KKRTC's, the same way it runs services into Kerala or Goa without those being KSRTC territory. Bus **class names** (Airavat, Rajahamsa, Pallakki, Ambaari) are shared across KSRTC/NWKRTC/KKRTC as a common branding scheme, which is itself part of why the operating corporation is not obvious to a rider — the livery and class name look identical regardless of which corporation actually owns the bus. [I]

**Does any consumer surface disclose the actual operator?** No dedicated confirmation found either way. What is established:
- The AWATAR reservation portal (ksrtc.in) is the *shared* booking backend for KSRTC + NWKRTC + KKRTC (+ some BMTC) [V, from KKRTC's own "AWATAR Booking and Counters" page], meaning a single website already spans all three RTCs — but its own terms & conditions page does not spell out how/whether the specific operating corporation is surfaced to the passenger at booking time. [S/I — read the T&C page directly; it describes the booking flow without mentioning operator disclosure]
- Third-party OTAs (RedBus, AbhiBus, Yatra) list an operator field of "KSRTC" generically for what one source describes as buses that in fact include NEKRTC/NWKRTC/BMTC fleet under that umbrella tag [S] — i.e., the retail brand "KSRTC" is being used as a catch-all for "Karnataka government bus" the way "Coke" gets used for any cola, which actively obscures rather than discloses the operating corporation.

**Conclusion for scoping**: this is exactly the kind of fact a Karnataka-wide journey planner should get right where existing surfaces don't — model the four RTCs as distinct operators with their own route/schedule data, but expect the underlying data sources (AWATAR, OTAs) to blur the distinction, so operator attribution will likely need cross-referencing depot/route metadata rather than trusting whatever operator label ships in a scraped listing.

## 3. Other public transport in Karnataka

### Hubballi-Dharwad BRTS ("Chigari")
- Operator: **jointly** Hubballi-Dharwad BRTS Company Limited (HDBRTS, a state SPV) and NWKRTC [S] — HDBRTS owns/manages the dedicated corridor infrastructure (stations, depots), NWKRTC supplies and operates the buses and crews.
- Corridor: 22.3 km dedicated busway [S]
- Stations: 32 median bus stations plus terminals/depots/workshops [S]
- Fleet: ~100 AC buses [S]
- Ridership: ~1,00,000/day [S]
- Cost: ₹971 crore project [S]
- Fare/ticketing: standard NWKRTC fare/ticketing infrastructure, not a separate fare system found in research [I]
- **Assessment**: Chigari is **not a distinct network requiring separate modelling** in the way BMRCL is. It is a defined corridor with its own dedicated stations and branding, operated with NWKRTC's fleet and (per available evidence) NWKRTC's fare/ticketing rails, and physically it's a fixed-route BRT with fixed stops — closer in modelling terms to a subset of NWKRTC city routes with dedicated infrastructure than to an independent operator. Recommend: model it as a tagged/branded subset of NWKRTC routes (stations = its own stop set, routes = NWKRTC schedules that use the corridor), not as a sixth operator.

### City bus services elsewhere
- **Mysuru**: KSRTC-operated, via a dedicated "Urban Division" out of Mysuru City Bus Stand — not a separate corporation, modelled as ordinary KSRTC routes tagged "city" [S/I]
- **Hubballi-Dharwad**: NWKRTC's Hubballi-Dharwad City division, including the Chigari BRTS [S]
- **Belagavi**: NWKRTC division-level city operation (Belagavi is one of NWKRTC's 9 divisions) [S/I]
- **Kalaburagi**: KKRTC-operated city service, branded "Nrupatunga Nagara Sarige" [S]
- **Davanagere, Shivamogga, Tumakuru**: KSRTC territory; city-level bus service exists but there's no dedicated "city corporation" or clearly separately branded city network found for these three the way Mysuru or Kalaburagi have one — treat their in-town services as ordinary KSRTC routes with in-city stage stops. [I — absence of evidence, not confirmed absence]
- **Mangaluru**: the outlier. City bus service is **dominated by private operators** (~325 buses) organized under the Dakshina Kannada Bus Operators' Association (DKBOA); KSRTC has only recently begun running some routes there too [S]. This is the one major city where the "which government corporation runs it" question doesn't have a clean answer — private operators are the primary city transit provider.

### Namma Metro lines — current/under-construction (2026)
Covered in §1. Summary: 3 open (Purple, Green, Yellow, 83 stations), Pink imminent (targeting Aug/Sep 2026 partial, Dec 2026 full), Blue/airport line under construction targeting March 2028.

### Bengaluru Suburban Rail (K-RIDE / BSRP)
- Implementing agency: Rail Infrastructure Development Company (Karnataka) Ltd (K-RIDE), a joint venture between the state government and Indian Railways [S]
- **Status as of Aug 2026: zero km operational** [S]. Four planned corridors (Mallige, Kanaka, Sampige, Parijaata); Mallige ~30% and Kanaka ~10% physical progress, the other two have no civil-works contract awarded yet. Original contractor L&T terminated mid-construction on two corridors in July 2025. Cost has risen from ₹15,767 cr to ₹16,876 cr [S].
- Realistic full-network timeline cited by reporting: 2029–2030 [S], well past the project's original 2026 deadline.
- **Recommendation**: out of scope for now on engineering grounds as much as data grounds — there is nothing running to model. Worth a placeholder in the data model (so it can be turned on without a redesign) but zero near-term modelling effort justified.

### Indian Railways within Karnataka
- Karnataka is served primarily by **South Western Railway** (zone HQ Hubballi), 3,721 km of route, 3 divisions (Hubli, Bengaluru, Mysuru), also reaching into AP/Telangana/Goa/TN/Maharashtra [S]. Parts of the state (coastal Karnataka via Konkan Railway, some border areas) fall outside SWR.
- **Is it in scope for a "one stop solution"?** This is a judgment call, not a fact to verify:
  - *For*: intercity rail (Bengaluru–Mysuru, Bengaluru–Hubballi, Mangaluru–Bengaluru via Hassan) genuinely competes with and complements KSRTC/NWKRTC/KKRTC long-distance routes on the same city pairs; a rider planning Bengaluru→Hubballi legitimately wants to compare bus vs. train. Excluding rail leaves the "one stop for all of Karnataka" claim visibly false for anyone who has ever taken a train.
  - *Against*: Indian Railways is a central-government network, not a Karnataka Government asset (unlike the four RTCs and BMRCL's state stake) — pulling it in changes the app's identity from "Karnataka's own transport, unified" to "national rail data vendor," a much bigger and differently-shaped data problem (real-time running status, PNR, tatkal, IRCTC integration politics) that dwarfs the RTC modelling work. It also isn't blocked on availability — the data exists richly (IRCTC, NTES, open rail APIs) — so the cost is integration/product-scope, not research.
  - **Recommendation**: treat rail as a **later-phase, explicitly separate module** — station-to-station journey suggestions and schedule display (using existing open rail data/APIs) rather than booking, so the app can tell a rider "there's also a train" without taking on IRCTC's own product surface. Don't block v1 on it; don't pretend v1 covers "all of Karnataka transport" without at least a rail-aware disclaimer.

### Mangaluru, coastal services, ferries, private buses
- Mangaluru city transit: private-operator dominated (DKBOA), KSRTC entering more recently [S] — this is a real gap for public-corporation-only modelling; the app needs either private-operator route data (harder to source, no AWATAR-style aggregator) or an explicit "city service not modelled" flag for Mangaluru.
- **Water Metro / coastal ferry**: planned, not operational. A ~₹180 crore Mangaluru Water Metro project (Malavoor–Jappinamogaru via Gurupur/Netravati rivers) and a broader coastal ferry network (Mangaluru to Maravanthe) are in planning/PPP-structuring stages [S]. **Not in scope** — nothing to model yet; revisit when a tender is awarded.
- **Private bus sector generally**: ~2.5 lakh private buses registered in Karnataka (2020 figure) [S], but stage-carriage permits (i.e., the fixed-route, fixed-stop city/mofussil service a journey planner would model) have been an **RTC monopoly since a March 2018 policy change** stopped new private stage-carriage permits statewide, with an exception carved out for **intra-district services in Dakshina Kannada and Udupi** [S] — which is exactly why Mangaluru (Dakshina Kannada) is the private-bus outlier. Elsewhere, private operators run contract carriages (tourist/charter, not fixed-route) which are out of scope for a fixed-route journey planner by definition. [I]

## 4. Fare and ticketing systems across the five

| System | Covers | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **AWATAR** | KSRTC, NWKRTC, KKRTC (NEKRTC), and "few services of BMTC" [S] | Shared advance-reservation portal/app, government-run. This is the closest thing to a unified booking backend across the four RTCs and could be a data source, though its own documentation doesn't confirm clean per-service operator disclosure (see §2 Hampi discussion). |
| **BMTC ticketing** | BMTC only | Namma BMTC app (tracking, fare calc), UPI QR at ETMs, TUMMOC app resells passes, NCMC tap-to-pay rolling out from ~June 2026 via new Chalo-built ETMs (11,000 units, ~₹30 cr four-year contract; pilot at Yeshwantpur/Subashnagar depots, expanding to Banashankari/Kengeri/Koramangala/Shivajinagar) [S] |
| **BMRCL ticketing** | Metro only | Contactless Smart Tokens (single journey), Contactless Smart Cards, QR-code mobile ticketing, NCMC (issued with RBL Bank since March 2023) [S] |
| **NCMC ("One Nation One Card")** | Metro since 2023; BMTC rolling out 2026 | Central scheme, not Karnataka-specific; **not yet extended to KSRTC/NWKRTC/KKRTC** in anything found — no evidence of NCMC acceptance on mofussil/intercity RTC buses. |
| **"One Karnataka" card** | Not found as a live, named program | Searches turned up only the generic NCMC rollout narrative under Bengaluru-specific headlines ("Bengaluru One Card"); no evidence of a dedicated cross-RTC "One Karnataka" mobility card distinct from NCMC. Treat as **not existing** rather than "found but undocumented." [I from absence] |
| **Shakti scheme** | Free travel for women/transgender residents on **BMTC, KSRTC, NWKRTC, KKRTC** ordinary/non-premium services [S] | Explicitly **excludes** all premium/AC/luxury classes: Rajahamsa, Vajra, Vayu Vajra, Airavat (all variants), Ambaari (all variants), non-AC sleeper, EV-Power Plus [S]. Does **not** apply to BMRCL (metro fares unaffected). By mid-2026, cited at >500 crore free trips cumulative, ~55–60 lakh women/day using it across the four corporations, with government reimbursements to the corporations reportedly lagging [S]. This is a major fare-computation wrinkle: the app needs to know per-route/per-class Shakti eligibility to show accurate net fares for a large share of riders. |

**Does unified ticketing already exist that the app would duplicate or could ride on?** Partially. AWATAR already unifies *booking* across three of the four RTCs (a real prior-art asset a journey planner could integrate against rather than re-scrape route data from scratch, if API access is obtainable — not confirmed whether AWATAR exposes a public API). NCMC is unifying *payment* between BMRCL and BMTC, but is a national scheme, not something Tatak would build, and doesn't yet reach the mofussil RTCs. There is no evidence of a single trip-planning surface spanning all five operators — that gap is real and is squarely the app's opportunity (see §5).

## 5. Prior art

- **Official KSRTC app (AWATAR)**: booking/reservation only, across KSRTC/NWKRTC/KKRTC (+ some BMTC). Not a journey planner — no multimodal routing, no BMTC city-route search, no metro integration. [S]
- **Namma BMTC app**: BMTC-only — live tracking, schedules, fare calculator. City-scoped, single-operator. [S]
- **Namma Metro / BMRCL's own channels**: ticketing (QR passes, smart card) for the metro only.
- **Namma Yatri**: originated as a driver-owned auto/cab-hailing app; has added metro schedule lookup, auto booking to/from metro stations, and (per Enroute MaaS Challenge coverage) multimodal trip suggestions integrating metro + bus + auto in Bengaluru [S]. Bengaluru-only; last-mile-and-hailing focused, not a route planner for the RTC network.
- **Tummoc**: multi-city Indian transit app (originally Kolkata-rooted); in Bengaluru offers metro + BMTC bus timing/route lookup and ticketing, plus the same MaaS Challenge multimodal integration with Namma Yatri for auto connectivity [S]. Bengaluru-scoped; no evidence of KSRTC/NWKRTC/KKRTC mofussil coverage.
- **Chalo**: the company behind BMTC's new ETM rollout and ticketing infrastructure (see §4) — an infrastructure/ticketing vendor to BMTC, not itself a consumer-facing statewide planner. It does operate its own city-transit apps in other Indian cities but no evidence found of a Chalo consumer app covering Karnataka's mofussil operators.
- **ONDC-based apps**: metro ticketing (not full journey planning) is live via ONDC across Bengaluru, Chennai, Delhi, Kochi, Pune, Nagpur and Mumbai, bookable through RedBus, EaseMyTrip, OneTicket, Highway Delite, Yatri, Miles & Kilometres, etc. [S] — this is a ticket-purchase network, not route planning, and again Bengaluru/metro-scoped within Karnataka.

**The gap**: every existing surface is either (a) single-operator (KSRTC's own app, BMTC's own app, BMRCL's own ticketing), or (b) Bengaluru-only even when multimodal (Namma Yatri, Tummoc), or (c) a booking/payment rail rather than a trip planner (AWATAR, ONDC, Chalo's ETMs). **Nobody has built cross-operator, statewide trip planning that spans BMTC + BMRCL + KSRTC + NWKRTC + KKRTC.** The specific rider need nobody serves today: "how do I get from anywhere in Karnataka to anywhere else in Karnataka, on public transport, regardless of which of the five government operators runs the relevant leg" — including telling the Bengaluru→Hampi rider what corporation they're actually boarding, something no surface found does today.

| **Mangaluru private-bus city network** | **M–L**, but **data-blocked**, possibly permanently | No AWATAR-equivalent exists for DKBOA's private operators; there may be no structured schedule data at all, only physical route knowledge. This may end up out of scope not by choice but because the data doesn't exist in ingestible form — worth an early spike to confirm before committing a unit of work. |
| **Davanagere/Shivamogga/Tumakuru in-city service** | **S**, low priority | Engineering-light if modelled simply as tagged KSRTC routes; the actual gap is that no distinctly-branded city network was found for these three, so there may be nothing extra to build beyond ordinary KSRTC route coverage. |
| **Hubballi-Dharwad/Belagavi city divisions (non-BRTS)** | **S** | Folds into the NWKRTC unit; no separate onboarding needed beyond flagging which NWKRTC routes are "city" vs. mofussil. |
| **Indian Railways (Karnataka intercity)** | **XL**, deliberately deferred | Not blocked on data availability (IRCTC/NTES/open rail APIs are rich) — blocked on product scope. Recommend explicit later phase, schedule-display only, not booking. |
| **Bengaluru Suburban Rail (K-RIDE)** | **—** (no unit yet) | Nothing operational to model; revisit when a corridor actually opens (earliest currently-cited target: 2029–2030 for full network, per reporting, though a first corridor could conceivably open sooner). |
| **Mangaluru/coastal ferry, Water Metro** | **—** (no unit yet) | Pre-tender planning stage; nothing to model. |

**Net recommendation on the three items the task specifically asked to weigh in on**:
- **Chigari BRTS**: belongs in scope, but as a *tagged subset of the NWKRTC dataset* (its own stations/stops, NWKRTC's routes and fleet) — not as a sixth independently-modelled operator. Low incremental cost once NWKRTC is onboarded.
- **Bengaluru Suburban Rail**: does not belong in scope yet on engineering grounds, because there is nothing running (zero km operational as of Aug 2026). Worth a schema placeholder, zero implementation effort now.
- **Indian Railways**: belongs in scope only as a deliberately separate, later-phase, schedule-awareness feature (not booking, not full integration) — include it for completeness of "how do I get from A to B" journey suggestions, but don't let it block or dilute the core four-RTC-plus-metro scope, since it's a different kind of network (central government, not Karnataka-owned) and a much bigger product surface than the road-transport modelling this app is actually named for.
